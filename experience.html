PhD candidate in Dr. Wissam Kontar's research team at the Civil & Environmental Engineering Department and Nebraska Transportation Center.

AV behavioral alignment

Measures how automated vehicles trade off risk, flow, and smoothness in naturalistic trajectory data (TGSIM), and builds the empirical frontier of what real driving achieves.

Learning-based AV control

Trains reinforcement learning policies in SUMO on high-performance computing, guided by those empirical trade-offs, and tests them on facility types held out of training.

Driver heterogeneity

Represents each driver as an evolving quantum state learned from trajectory data (I-24 MOTION, TGSIM), capturing differences between drivers and changes over time.

Interacting agents

Develops quantum models of agents with memory and group effects, at the level of individual drivers and of neighborhoods of vehicles.

Travel behavior

Contributes to quantum models of dynamic travel behavior, applied to activity and mobility data.

Safety and exposure
