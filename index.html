§01 · About

About

Mohammad Elayan is a PhD candidate in Civil Engineering (Transportation) at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln and a member of the Transportation and Autonomy Laboratory for Intelligence, directed by Dr. Wissam Kontar.

His research develops models of driver behavior and interaction learned from data rather than fixed in advance, and uses them to make automated vehicles balance competing objectives among drivers who all behave differently. His work spans quantum models of behavior and interaction, behavioral alignment and learning-based control for automated vehicles, traffic flow, traffic safety with emerging data, and automated mobility for underserved communities. It has appeared in Transportation Research Part C, Transportation Research Record, the Journal of Transportation Engineering, and at IEEE ITSC, IEEE IV, and the TRB Annual Meeting, with a Best Paper Award shortlisting at IEEE ITSC 2026.

Before his doctorate, he spent ten years as a transportation engineer and senior transportation engineer in Dubai, contributing to 111 projects across the UAE and MENA region and managing 63 of them, from traffic impact studies to transportation master plans and multimodal integration. That practice is where his research questions began.

§02 · Research

Research Interests

My research learns how people drive, travel, and interact from real data, and uses it to build automated vehicles and transportation systems that work with people as they are.

Fig. 1 · AV Control

Multi-Objective AV Control

Measuring how automated vehicles balance safety, traffic flow, and smoothness in real driving, building the empirical frontier of what is achievable, and training AV control toward it, aware of the drivers around them.

Fig. 2 · Behavior

Driver and Travel Behavior Modeling

Representing each driver or traveler as an evolving quantum state learned from data, so that differences between people, and changes in each person over time and context, are captured rather than averaged away.

Fig. 3 · Interaction

Interaction with Memory and Group Effects

Modeling how agents respond to their recent history and to their neighbors, both one by one and as a group, at the level of individual drivers and of whole neighborhoods of vehicles.

Fig. 4 · Traffic Flow

Traffic Flow and Simulation

Connecting individual behavior to traffic phenomena such as stop-and-go waves, hysteresis, and the fundamental diagram, and building behavior models compact enough to run for every vehicle in a simulation.

Fig. 5 · Safety

Traffic Safety with Smart Data

Surrogate safety measures computed from trajectories, and crash models that draw on location-based services data, street-level imagery, and the built environment to locate risk at fine resolution.

Fig. 6 · Access

Automated Mobility and Access

Using geospatial analysis to find underserved communities, such as rural pharmaceutical deserts, and optimization to plan how conventional and autonomous delivery can reach them.
